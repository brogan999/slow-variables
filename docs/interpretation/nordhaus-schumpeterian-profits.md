# How much of the gain the innovator keeps

A note on how the tracker reads William Nordhaus's "Schumpeterian Profits in the American Economy: Theory and Measurement" (NBER working paper, 2004). It organises what the tracker measures; it supplies no figures.

**The question.** Nordhaus asks what fraction of the social value created by innovation is captured as profit by the innovators, and answers it by comparing the surplus an innovation creates with the returns its producers record. The striking finding of that paper is how small the captured fraction is: most of the value leaks to consumers as lower prices or better products.

**Why the tracker cares.** The diffusion lens and the capture lens are the two halves of this question. If AI follows the pattern Nordhaus found, a fast-moving capability frontier is consistent with thin and temporary profits, and the interesting number is not how capable the models are but how long any layer holds its margin. The thesis monitor's "consumers keep the surplus" condition is this paper's claim turned into something falsifiable: it holds while measured consumer surplus runs ahead of the revenue the sellers book.

**The measurement problem, stated honestly.** Nordhaus works from national accounts and a model of the counterfactual; the tracker cannot. What it has instead is willingness-to-pay evidence on one side and filed revenue and run-rates on the other, which is why the surplus test is bracketed rather than computed: it fails below a floor, holds above a ceiling, and is untestable in between. The bracket is the tracker's admission that the middle of Nordhaus's calculation is not available from free sources.

**Rents are temporary by construction.** In this account a profit is the reward for being ahead, and it decays as imitation arrives. That is why the tracker scores direction and half-lives rather than levels: a layer's margin today says less than whether it is holding, and a price that halves at a fixed capability is the decay showing up on the buyer's side.
