# Stocks, flows and leverage points

A note on how the tracker's diffusion diagram reads through Donella Meadows' systems thinking, from her essay "Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System" (Sustainability Institute, 1999) and her book *Thinking in Systems* (2008). It organises what the tracker measures; it supplies no figures.

**Stocks and flows.** The five buckets (methods, products, early adoption, adaptation, and the return arrow) are stocks: accumulations that change only through their inflows and outflows. The valves between them are the flows. A stock can be large while its flow is slow, which is why the tracker scores valves and not only levels: a high share of firms "using AI" is a stock, and the rate at which that use becomes reorganised work is the flow that matters for the normal-technology question.

**Delays.** Meadows ranks the length of delays above most parameters as a place where systems resist change. Narayanan and Kapoor's case is largely a claim about delays: organisations, rules and complementary investments take years to adjust, so a fast upstream stock does not quickly fill the downstream ones. The adaptation bucket and the labour trackers are the tracker's reading of those delays.

**Balancing loops.** Safety brakes, backlash and rules act as balancing loops on deployment (the diagram's B1), and moving benchmark goalposts act as one on perceived progress (B2). Meadows notes that the strength of a balancing loop depends on how quickly and accurately it senses the gap it corrects, which is why the tracker records brake events as dated evidence rather than sentiment.

**Reinforcing loops.** The return arrow is a reinforcing loop: deployment produces data, revenue and agent labour that feed back into methods. Its gain, how much each round of deployment speeds the next round of invention, is what the continual-learning ladder, the refresh cadence and the research-acceleration series try to observe.

**Where the tracker is weak.** Meadows places parameters (prices, rates, counts) at the bottom of her list of leverage points, and information flows, rules, goals and paradigms near the top. Most of the tracker's indicators are parameters and delays, because those are what free sources publish. Loop gains are observed only through vendors' and labs' own descriptions, and shifts in rules or goals show up as evidence rows rather than as series. That imbalance is a limit of the instrument, stated here so it is not mistaken for a finding.
