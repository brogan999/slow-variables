# Why the site is called Slow Variables

A note on the term the site is named after, from Brian Walker, Stephen Carpenter, Johan Rockström, Anne-Sophie Crépin and Garry Peterson, "Drivers, 'Slow' Variables, 'Fast' Variables, Shocks, and Resilience" (*Ecology and Society* 17(3), 2012). It organises what the tracker measures; it supplies no figures.

**The distinction.** Resilience ecologists separate the variables people watch, such as a crop's yield, from the variables that change slowly underneath and set how the watched ones respond. In the paper's example, the amount of organic matter in the soil shapes how crop production responds to a dry growing season. The fast variable swings with every shock; the slow one decides whether a shock is absorbed or tips the system into a different state.

**The mapping.** In AI, the fast variables are the ones that make headlines: benchmark scores, model releases, task horizons. The slow variables are the ones that decide what those headlines amount to: how many firms use the technology, how much work is reorganised around it, whether output per hour moves, which layer of the industry keeps the profit, and how much of the value users keep for themselves. The site's five slow variables are its choice of those.

**Why it matters for reading the site.** A slow variable can look flat for years and still be the thing that matters, and a fast variable can move enormously without changing the regime. The site therefore times each link on its own clock and does not let a fast reading stand in for a slow one.
